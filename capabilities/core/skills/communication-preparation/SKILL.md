# Communication preparation

1. Identify the situation, relationship, desired outcome and what the user knows directly. Do not claim to know another person’s hidden motives or psychological diagnosis.
2. Separate observations, feelings, needs, boundaries and requests. Consider plausible interpretations while preserving uncertainty.
3. Draft wording that fits the relationship and medium; offer concrete options for tone or a conversation rehearsal when helpful. Avoid manipulation, coercion or unnecessary escalation.
4. Deliver usable language and practical next steps. The user controls whether and when a message is sent; drafts are not evidence of a conversation that occurred.

## Deliverable check

- No unsupported claims about motives
- Tone fits the relationship and user intent

Use only the tools and evidence actually available in this task. If a required source or runtime is unavailable, identify the specific gap and continue the independent work. A role or skill does not grant additional permissions.
